Josh

Josh McDermott

First, thank you to Josh McDermott, without whom this thesis would not exist. Through Josh I entered an intellectual world in which studying perception is a way of understanding reality itself. I will always cherish the early years of bringing new illusions to his office and sharing the excitement whenever we got close to a real effect. I am grateful for his commitment to the scientific process and to the training of his students. We spent countless hours editing text, checking analyses and rehearsing talks, until complex ideas were disentangled, every claim had been verified three times over, and as many alternative explanations as we could think of had been tested. He gave a great deal of himself to shape this work, and I learned from watching how he did it.

He was also patient with me while I worked through my family's situation and my finances, and I thank him for that support. I placed a great deal of trust in Josh during these years. The risk of trusting someone that much is that when I sensed I was not doing well, I felt like giving up on being an academic. The gift of it is that when he said something was possible, his words carried the same weight, and they carried me to the end of the PhD and into wanting to continue on this path. I am grateful for the trust we placed in each other. He works incredibly hard, and my wish for him is that he achieves everything he hopes for scientifically, and that he then has more time to rest and to enjoy the things in life he cherishes.

Eric

Eric Martinez

To Eric. Although our paths crossed constantly, I feel I truly met you only twice, once in my first year and again in the hallway between the McDermott and Sinha labs. El primero, un desencuentro. El segundo, un encuentro. That second meeting was an unexpected and life-shifting arrival.

By your side, I have been unafraid to not know things. When I confessed why my shoelaces were so often undone, you taught me to swoop, loop and pull at the late age of twenty-seven. I remember the joy of being met with care instead of embarrassment, and that joy opened the door to everything that followed. I have been learning that same lesson ever since, in how I communicate, in how I listen to myself, in how I do my work.

I wonder how many people can feel the vastness in your "hmm, interesting," and in the question that follows, one that shows you have held space for every phrase and all its possible meanings. You extend this to everyone who speaks with you, but for someone who has rarely felt heard, it is a gift to be on the receiving end. It has taught me to take my own thoughts as seriously as you do, and that has shifted my world.

As I began to do that, I started taking my dreams more seriously too. On the famous blue slide, you asked me why I studied neuroscience, and I explained that I wanted to understand why empathy could so easily be absent in so many people. You asked whether I thought I was doing that now. It certainly isn't what this thesis is about, but it will be what my next ten papers are about. I owe that turn to the weight you give my words, and to the way you make not yet knowing how to do something feel expansive and full of possibility. [All with your…] I came here wanting to understand why empathy is so often absent, and along the way you have shown me what it feels like when it is fully present.

Thank you for believing in my project ideas, for helping me see situations from a fairer perspective so I could stand up for myself, and for reminding me my thesis was interesting whenever I lost sight of it. Thank you for asking me to present it to you over dinner. The seafood was expensive, but it gave shape to my defense.

I am amazed by how much you shape my inner life, even though we live mostly at a distance and spend only a small part of our time together. With you I have felt the complete absence of loneliness, in my work and science and in my adventurous explorations of my emotional and creative life.

To many more swoops, loops and pulls.

Mom

Sehila, my mom

And finally, to my mom, Sehila. There was a time when I thought you would not be here, in that audience or at all, and I am so grateful for the strength it took. I think of the courage it took to live in Cambridge, and of all you have done in these two years. The more I learn about everything you have been through, the more the PhD looks like the easy part. You make it seem like the easiest thing in the world.

As I close this chapter, one I have been pursuing since long before the PhD began, I keep thinking about all the other paths my life could have taken. Some of them lead to dark places. Instead, I get to be here, and I do not think that is luck. Along the way I met people with the kindest hearts and the most thoughtful minds, and their generosity let me keep my heart open, to not become cynical or cold, and to stay close to my humanity and to what matters about community. It is because I met all of you that I kept believing in the goodness of people, and it is what makes me want to pass on the light you have given me. Thank you for keeping my heart open.
